# Voice guide (example — replace with your own)

This file teaches the caption writer how you sound. It is the single highest-leverage file in
the repo: the pipeline can find your best photos, but it cannot guess your voice.

Build it from real material — your own past captions, messages you've written, and your edits
to drafts. A guide written from imagination produces captions that sound like a brand account.

## Who's posting

One paragraph: who you are, what you do, what you care about, who reads this.

> Example: "Alex is a nurse who runs long distances and cooks too ambitiously for a weeknight.
> Posts for friends, not an audience. Reads as warm and a little tired."

## The core rule

One sentence that captures the voice. Make it specific enough to reject a draft.

> Example: "Say the plain thing, then add one real detail. Never build a crafted punchline."

## Rejected vs. yours

The most useful section. Put a drafted caption next to how you'd actually write it. Five or six
rows teach more than any amount of description.

| Rejected (too written) | How I'd say it |
|---|---|
| Golden hour on the ridge, every step earned. | Got up the hill. Legs are done. |
| A bowl that took three hours and was worth every minute. | this took three hours and I'd do it again |

## What the voice does

Three to six bullets. Punctuation habits, capitalisation, emoji, length, whether you joke.

## Per platform

If you post differently in different places, say how. One line each.

## Never post

Topics that are off limits regardless of how good the photo is — money, health details, other
people's faces without their say-so, live locations, anything about family. Be concrete; this
list is enforced by you, not by the code.
